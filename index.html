The Gap

Access to Pakistan's financial system is the exception, not the rule.

Based on 1,000 individually surveyed adults in the Global Findex 2024 Pakistan dataset — a nationally representative sample of who banks, who borrows, who saves, and who is shut out entirely.

68.2% of adults surveyed have no account of any kind — no bank, no mobile money
75% of the least digitally-engaged group — found by the data itself, without being told to look for gender — are women
16.7% of excluded people could not raise emergency funds at all, vs. just 1.3% of the most digitally active group
What Drives It

The same two things predict adoption for almost everyone.

A Random Forest model, explained with SHAP, was used to rank which factors most strongly predict a person's level of financial engagement — and the ranking barely changes across gender, region, or behavioural group.

01

Phone access is the strongest predictor, everywhere

Whether someone owns no phone, a basic phone, or a smartphone outranks every other factor tested — income, education, and age included — for men and women, rural and urban respondents alike.

02

Being in the workforce is the second-strongest driver

People earning an income, formally or informally, are consistently more likely to hold and use financial accounts — a pattern that held across every subgroup tested.

03

What differs between groups isn't the driver — it's the outcome

The barriers are the same for everyone. What differs sharply is who actually clears them. That's the real story this project is built around.

Beyond The Numbers

Two barriers rarely show up in an access statistic.

Findex asks people directly why they don't have a phone or an account. Their own answers tell a fuller story than "no access" alone.

Family & friends

The real safety net isn't a bank — it's people

Across every adoption tier, 43–50% of respondents name family or friends as their main source of emergency funds — far ahead of savings or formal loans, even among the most digitally advanced group. Formal fintech in Pakistan sits on top of an informal system that already does most of the work.

Why women lack phones

Money is the biggest reason. It isn't the only one.

43.8% of women without a phone cite affordability — but 22.8% cite family or community disapproval, and 17.8% cite safety concerns. Closing the gap needs more than cheaper devices.

The cost of being included

Digital engagement brings real fraud exposure

30.4% of the more digitally-engaged group report a scam or phishing attempt via phone — compared to just 5.0% of the less-engaged group. Getting people online isn't the finish line.

The Method

Built to be checked, not just believed.

Every number on this page comes from a documented, reproducible pipeline — supervised prediction, independently-validated clustering, and formal statistical testing, not a single model run.
