The Gap

Two behavioural segments emerged from the data — without gender ever being an input.

Based on Global Findex Pakistan microdata — a nationally representative survey of who banks, who borrows, who saves, and who is shut out entirely. K-means clustering was run purely on demographic and access variables, with no group labels supplied in advance.

43.1% of respondents fall into a cluster the model itself labelled "Digitally Excluded" — 96.3% of whom are women
44.3% fall into a "Connected but Unengaged" cluster — just 16.3% of whom are women
V = 0.807 Cramér's V — a very strong statistical association between cluster membership and gender, not a coincidence
What Drives It

The same two things predict adoption for almost everyone.

A Random Forest model, explained with SHAP, was used to rank which factors most strongly predict a person's level of financial engagement — and the ranking barely changes across gender, region, or behavioural group.

01

Phone access is the strongest predictor, everywhere

Whether someone owns no phone, a basic phone, or a smartphone outranks every other factor tested — income, education, and age included — for men and women, rural and urban respondents alike.

02

Being in the workforce is the second-strongest driver

People earning an income, formally or informally, are consistently more likely to hold and use financial accounts — a pattern that held across every subgroup tested.

03

What differs between groups isn't the driver — it's the outcome

The barriers are similar for everyone. What differs sharply is who actually clears them. That's the real story this project is built around.

The Method

Built to be checked, not just believed.

Every number on this page comes from a documented, reproducible pipeline — tuned and cross-validated models, independently-stability-tested clustering, and formal statistical significance testing, not a single model run.

80.0% XGBoost test accuracy (macro-F1 0.481) predicting adoption tier from demographic & access features
0.494 macro-F1 for the tuned, cross-validated Random Forest — hyperparameters selected via RandomizedSearchCV
p = 0.0001 chi-square significance for the urban/rural association with adoption tier

In progress

Why, not just who: self-reported reasons & coping mechanisms

Findex asks people directly why they lack a phone or an account, and what they actually do when they need emergency money. This next phase analyses those answers directly, to move from "what predicts exclusion" to "what excluded people say about their own situation."

In progress

A financial planning & education tool, built on these findings

An application that uses this project's model to estimate a person's likely financial engagement level and explain the factors behind it — paired with general financial education content, not personalised advice. Built to be used safely by exactly the population this research is about.
